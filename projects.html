DM TELEVISION

The Dark Matter TEsts using LEVItated Sensor Interferometer Observatory Network (DM Television) is a gravitational wave (GW) detector which uses optically levitated microparticles as resonant sensors for sensing small variations in the spacetime curvature. Previously, LIGO has detected these variations in spacetime occurring at frequencies from 10 Hz to few kHz. Those “ripples” in spacetime were generated typically by two compact objects (black holes or neutron stars) inspiraling around each other and eventually colliding.

The upcoming LISA satellite observatory will look for similar GWs at lower frequencies. However, this leaves an unexplored region at higher frequencies. Novel sources such as sub-solar mass primordial black holes, as well as gravitationally bound states of the QCD axion, formed via black hole superradiance, may reside here. This project aims to explore that region, from 10 kHz, up to a few hundred kHz.

The UC Davis detector is part of a two-node observatory network, similar to LIGO Hanford and Livingston, with the other node at Northwestern University.

ARIADNE Experiment

The Axion Resonant InterAction DetectioN Experiment (ARIADNE) looks for the QCD axion mediating a spin-dependent force between a mass and a spin. The QCD axion is one of the leading candidates for dark matter , primarily because it also solves an unrelated problem in particle physics, the Strong CP problem.

ARIADNE is searching for a new force mediated by the QCD axion. This force couples a spin and mass via an effective magnetic field that depends on the separation between the mass and spin. Therefore, a periodic change in the separation will modulate this effective magnetic field. This modulation can be measured via nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR); i.e. preparing the spin in a constant magnetic field and modulating the effective field in a transverse direction perpendicular to the bias field. We use a tungsten rotor as the source mass, and hyperpolarized 3He gas as a spin ensemble in this experiment. As the tungsten source mass rotates, the mass (and hence the magnetic field) seen by the 3He spins gets modulated. If this modulation occurs at the Larmor frequency, the spins acquire a slight transverse magnetization, which we then pick up via a SQUID.
