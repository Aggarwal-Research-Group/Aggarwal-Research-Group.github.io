PI

Nancy Aggarwal

Professor Nancy Aggarwal did her undergraduate studies at IIT Bombay majoring in Engineering Physics with honors in Physics and a minor in Electrical Engineering. She then went to MIT to conduct research on the LIGO detector for her PhD. Her focus was to improve the quantum measurement uncertainty in gravitational wave detectors using specially engineered quantum states of light, for example squeezed states. After her PhD, she joined Northwestern University as a joint postdoctoral fellow at the Center for Fundamental Physics and Center for Interdisciplinary and Exploratory Research in Astrophysics. She started as an assistant professor at UC Davis in 2023. Aggarwal’s interests lie in using precision measurements to probe topics in fundamental physics, for example gravitational waves, dark matter, and studies of the quantum nature of gravity. She is a member of the LIGO Scientific Collaboration, LSD Collaboration, and ARIADNE Collaboration. She is also a co-founder of an international initiative called Ultra-High Frequency Gravitational Wave Initiative which brings together community members interested in ultra-high-frequency GW detection through regular workshops and review articles.

Graduate Students

Jackson Larsen

Jackson Larsen, completing his undergraduate studies at Western Washington University with a major in Physics and Minor in both Astronomy and Mathematics, began his PhD at UC Davis in 2024. Coming in with a background in precision optical measurements as a LIGO affiliate, he is currently working under the DM Television experiment, building the interferometer system used to detect incoming gravitational waves. For hobbies, he enjoys regularly backpacking, as well as reading obscure sci-fi novels.

Dillon Goulart

Dillon's undergraduate career began with his associate's degree in physics at Ohlone College in Fremont. Here, he attended several programs, including NASA's NCAS, MCA, and NPWEE, which culminated in a NASA Lucy mission summer internship focused on the Trojan asteroids. He concluded with two Bachelor's degrees from the University of California, Berkeley, in Astrophysics and Physics. Here, he had experience as one of the first undergraduate physics peer mentors and primarily worked with Dr. Karl Van Bibber, searching for axion-like dark matter. He worked with DMRadio, ALPHA, and HAYSTAC R&D, primarily with tuning mechanisms and cavity characterization. Dillon published two papers about the haloscope projects, one as the first author and the other as the second author. He has many interests in novel methods searching for dark matter and is currently helping with the ARIADNE collaboration search for dark matter using NMR.
